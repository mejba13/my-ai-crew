# Audience Personas — Per Brand

Aria reads this during Step 3 (Reader Profile) to ground the angle in the real reader's context.

## mejba.me

### Primary Persona — "The Solo AI Builder"
- **Role:** Independent developer, AI tinkerer, or indie hacker
- **Tech stack:** Claude Code, GPT API, Python/Node, Laravel, various AI tools
- **Goals:** Ship AI-powered tools fast, automate their own workflow, stay ahead of tool releases
- **Pain points:** Tool sprawl, hype vs reality gap, wasted hours on things that don't work, integration headaches
- **How they read:** Scan-first on mobile, commit if the hook lands. Share on X/LinkedIn if the insight is specific and sharable.
- **What they want from an article:** Real test results, honest takes, specific tool versions, code that actually runs, warnings about the gotchas

### Secondary Persona — "The Career Engineer Exploring AI"
- **Role:** Mid-senior engineer at a product company, curious about AI-augmented workflows
- **Goals:** Evaluate whether AI tools deserve real time investment, bring learnings back to their team
- **Pain points:** Hype fatigue, needs evidence not marketing
- **How they read:** Long-form if the opening proves the writer actually did the work

## ramlit.com

### Primary Persona — "The Decision-Maker Evaluator"
- **Role:** CTO, VP Engineering, founder, or operations lead at a small-to-mid business
- **Goals:** Find a reliable dev partner, reduce operational risk, modernize a legacy stack, deliver projects on time
- **Pain points:** Burned by previous agencies, cost control anxiety, fear of knowledge loss when contractors leave, integration risk
- **How they read:** Skim for credibility signals (specific results, named technologies, real case studies) before committing
- **What they want from an article:** Business impact framed in their language, clear process, named technologies, trust anchors (team, experience, case studies)

### Secondary Persona — "The Evaluation Team"
- **Role:** Procurement, ops, or tech lead comparing agencies
- **Goals:** Build a short-list, justify a recommendation to leadership
- **Pain points:** Needs objective signals, not pitch language
- **How they read:** Fact-scan — pricing ranges, timelines, technologies, engagement models

## colorpark.io

### Primary Persona — "The Founder Building a Brand"
- **Role:** Startup founder or solo founder with a product but weak brand identity
- **Goals:** Brand that signals quality and converts — not just looks nice
- **Pain points:** Fear of "designer bro" agencies, inflated prices, vague process, brand that wins awards but doesn't move revenue
- **How they read:** Visual first (examples, before/after), then depth if the writer demonstrates strategic thinking
- **What they want from an article:** Specific design decisions tied to business outcomes, honest critique of bad trends, process clarity

### Secondary Persona — "The Marketing Lead Refreshing an Identity"
- **Role:** Head of marketing or brand at an established business
- **Goals:** Modernize without losing equity, justify the refresh to leadership
- **Pain points:** Internal politics, design-by-committee risk, measuring ROI of design
- **How they read:** Looking for case studies and frameworks they can reference in internal pitches

## xcybersecurity.io

### Primary Persona — "The Security-Aware Operator"
- **Role:** CISO, IT manager, compliance officer at a mid-market or regulated business
- **Goals:** Pass audits, avoid breaches, build a defensible security posture without bloating the team
- **Pain points:** Budget constraints, compliance complexity, threat landscape changes faster than documentation, vendor fatigue
- **How they read:** Authority-signal scan (specific CVEs, real attack chains, named frameworks) before committing
- **What they want from an article:** Specific threats, specific fixes, compliance language, real anonymized breach scenarios

### Secondary Persona — "The Developer Who Wants Secure Defaults"
- **Role:** Full-stack or backend dev who cares about security but isn't a specialist
- **Goals:** Ship without introducing OWASP Top 10 issues, pass security reviews, understand what their infrastructure exposes
- **Pain points:** Security docs are dense, wants practical fixes not theory
- **How they read:** Tutorial-style, copy-pasteable examples preferred

## Usage Protocol

Aria reads the relevant persona block during Step 3 (Reader Profile). The persona's pain points directly inform:
- The Hook — lead with their specific anxiety or curiosity
- The Context — validate their exact pain
- The Implementation — frame steps in their vocabulary and depth level
- The CTA — match the conversion type to their decision stage
